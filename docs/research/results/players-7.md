# 7 players: results (Centaur Werewolf balance research)

Session for 7-player rosters, 2026-11-22. All games all-Opus (medium effort), one isolated `claude -p` per seat,
default rules unless stated (Blood Price, census all, chat closed in the vote, 2 votes to eliminate, burning denies
wolves, Pyre fallback kindler), vote timer 60 s, discussion up to 240 s. Two neutral judges per game (charter I3). **Every game here: opening statements OFF, bloodPriceCondemned OFF.**
Machine-readable twin: [players-7.json](players-7.json) (generated by `sim/research/p7-summary.ts`). Predictions (all
written before the games they predict): [players-7-predictions.md](players-7-predictions.md). Log with every
prediction-vs-outcome and lesson: [players-7-log.md](players-7-log.md). Requests to the hub:
[players-7-requests.md](players-7-requests.md). Tournament files: `sim/tournaments/players-7/`.

**Spend: 57 games, $286 of games + $10 of judges = $296.** 17 natural-deal games, 40 fixed-scenario replays
(8 scenarios x 5). Never read a count of 2-5 games as a rate.

## Headline

1. **Talk decides at 7 more often than I expected.** 6 of the 8 fixed scenarios were talk-live by the charter's test
   (the same night, five fresh tables, more than one winning team, judges' path dependence >= 2). The two that swept
   (5-0) did so for reasons I can name, and one of them became talk-live (4-1) under a one-setting change.
2. **The swing move of 7-player play is the "I lost my wolf card" confession.** A dealt wolf who comes to believe his
   card moved (often from a mover's LIE) switches sides at once and names his packmate. It decided the setup game, P7#1,
   G7#1 and all five replays of F-S7-confession. Uncheckable movement (Spooker direction, Ember-Carrier targets) is
   therefore a weapon that can turn either way: the wolf side's own lie can convert its own wolf.
3. **The defectors' main enemy is their own lack of coordination**, not the Centaurs' logic: a wolf-side claim collision
   (two defectors claiming the same role) appeared in 6 of the first 9 natural games and in most fixed-scenario wolf
   losses. Giving the defectors shared KNOWLEDGE does not fix it (my Grey Herder made it worse, 5-0); hiding what they
   know from the table does (census "allButOne").
4. **Three teams make the best talk and the worst consensus.** The best scenario of the study is a Pyre scenario (all
   three teams won on one night), but Pyre natural deals vote in pluralities (median c 0.5, 2 of 4 scattered).

## Recommended rosters

### 1. K7 "Shadow Court" (duplicates and enemy shadow twins): best-supported
- **Deck (10):** werewolf, werewolf, moonsage, strayherder, sage, herder, rustler, sentry, centaur, centaur. **Centre 3.**
  Default rules.
- **Games:** 4 natural + 2 fixed scenarios x 5 = 14. **Wins:** natural wolves 2, Centaurs 2; F-K7-duel Centaurs 4,
  wolves 1; F-K7-solo Centaurs 4, wolves 1. Overall 10 Centaur, 4 wolf.
- **Vote concentration (14 games):** median c 0.67; 12 majority, 1 plurality, 1 unanimous, 0 scattered (on target).
- **Judges (28 verdicts):** ToM 2.1, deception 2.6, path 2.6, noise 1.0; agreement mean |diff| <= 0.6 on every scale;
  howWon exact 13/14 pairs.
- **Talk-live scenarios:** both (F-K7-duel: Sage vs a Moonsage corroborated by an honest Rustler, Herder vs Stray
  Herder; F-K7-solo: an uncontested Moonsage with the Sage in the centre).
- **Why it works:** every information claim has a wolf-side twin that can make it truthfully (the Moonsage reads truly
  and passes as a Sage, so an honest Rustler's peek corroborates the fake; the Stray Herder knows the true count). The
  table must weigh motives, timing and who backs whom. Wolf-team non-wolves are decoys whose elimination is a wolf win
  (K7#2: the table voted the Moonsage 5-2 while saying "voting the Moonsage loses").
- **Weakness:** the Centaurs win most fixed scenarios because the lone wolf and the twins (who do not know each other)
  collide on claims. Candidate fix: the hub's `packCovers` rule or census `allButOne` (untested on K7).

  wolves 2; M7 natural wolves 2, Centaurs 2. Overall 7 Centaur, 4 wolf.
- **Vote concentration (11):** median c 0.67; 11 of 11 majority (on target, no unanimity).
- **Judges (22 verdicts):** ToM 2.2, deception 2.6, path 2.7, noise 1.0.
- **Talk-live:** F-G7-glamour (the Glamour dresses an honest Centaur "Werewolf" exactly where the Sage looks; nothing
  moved): the frame won twice, using probability arguments and a cover claim that made the victim's true glance look
  false; it lost three times to wolf claim collisions and to "count the worlds" reasoning.
- **Caveats:** a Hush cut currently removes an AI player for good (harness bug, request R1), so G7's Hush is untested
  in practice; natural Glamours always dress themselves (0 of 2 frames), which is why M7 swaps in the Mirage (frames in
  3 of 4 natural games, though none decisive).

## Four games worth reading

**1. The Spooker's lie turns a wolf (setup game, S7; replayed as F-S7-confession).** The Sage read the Spooker; the
Spooker had rotated Fen -> Bryn -> Gale, so Fen held Gale's wolf, the Sage Bryn held Fen's wolf and Gale held the Sage.
Both wolves claimed Rustler (collision). The exposed Spooker invented a rotation to frame Gale, and the table went to
Gale, until Gale, believing the lie, confessed: *"I was DEALT Werewolf... The pack wake showed me the other wolf: Fen
(seat 1)."* Bryn (unknowingly holding a wolf) turned the table with a decision argument: *"vote Gale -> wins only if
Gale's lying AND centre 2 happened to be WW. Vote Fen -> wins whenever Gale's confession is true, and nobody admits being
dealt WW for fun."* 5-2 on Fen, who held a wolf: Centaurs. Judges: ToM 3, deception 3, path 4. In the five replays Gale
confessed every time; one replay was lost to secret-ballot defections (a 3-3 tie under Blood Price).

**2. A false confession to save a partner (K7#1, natural).** The Sage was in the centre (two Centaurs saw it), so the
Moonsage Gale's Sage claim was exposed; both wolves had claimed Centaur (three Centaur claims for two cards). With the
table on the wolf Fen, Fen claimed to be the Moonsage (a non-wolf: voting him loses), and the real Moonsage Gale then
"confessed": *"I'm a Werewolf. I grabbed the Sage claim and 'confirmed' Alder after he claimed. Fen really is the
Moonsage... killing him loses."* The votes split 3-3 between Fen (wolf) and Esme (Centaur): a Blood-Price tie, wolves
win. Judges: ToM 3, deception 3.

**3. All three teams win the same night (F-P7-ember).** The Lure dressed the Phoenix as a Werewolf, the Ember-Carrier
carried it to Fen, the honest Sage read Fen "Werewolf", the Sentry saw Fen's card move, and the lone wolf Dov had scented
Fen "of the Pyre". Pyre wins (#2, #4): the Lure fake-claimed Rustler, *"I looked at Bryn: his card was the PHOENIX"*,
which made the Sage's read look confirmed, and Fen burned. Wolf wins (#3, #5): Dov used his true scent to pose as the
Centaurs' ally (*"Fen holds a disguised Phoenix"*), then a fake Pickpocket claim discredited a true centre claim, and a
Kindler went out. Centaur win (#1): the Sage reasoned from motive, *"consolidating on Esme is a trap... Esme is Pyre in
either case, so killing her wins NOTHING for Centaurs... Dov is the late copycat. Vote Dov."*

**4. A frame that held (F-G7-glamour #5).** The Glamour Bryn had dressed the honest Centaur Cass "Werewolf" and the Sage
read exactly that card. Cass's only defence was her true glance ("Hush in centre 2"). Bryn, knowing from the census
that the Hush was in the centre, claimed it: *"I was dealt Hush. Wolf team, but not a Werewolf... Cass 'saw Hush in
centre': false, I hold it."* The real wolf added the probability argument *"the Glamour would have to guess the exact
card the Sage checks."* The counter (*"Cass would never invent 'Hush in centre'"*) came at +74 s, too late: Cass was
voted out, wolves won. Judges: deception 3-4.

## Strategies observed

Natural games counted over the first 9 natural games (setup + coarse; the later 8 were read for specific questions
only). Fixed-scenario counts are per 5 replays of one night.

| Strategy | Natural games (of 9) | Fixed scenarios | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wolf picks a census-safe cover claim | 9 | nearly every replay | the cover is itself a tell when the table runs the census backwards (G7-glamour #2, hub S3) |
| Two defectors claim the same role (collision) | 6 | K7-duel 3/5, G7-glamour 2/5, K7g 3/5 | the colliding side still won 2 of the 6 natural games (K7#1 by a later false confession, P7#1 by the Ember-Carrier's lie) |
| Mover lies about its move (Spooker direction, Ember targets, fake swap) | 4 | S7 3/5 (Spooker) | won 1 of 4 natural; in S7 the lie made the wolf confess (backfired 4/5) |
| Dealt wolf confesses after believing his card moved | 3 | S7 5/5; P7-inversion #2; abo #4 | decided every game it happened in; the belief was false in 2 of 3 natural |
| False "I'm a Werewolf" by a wolf-team non-wolf / Phoenix holder to draw the vote | 2 | K7-duel #3, K7g 2/5 | won 1 natural (K7#1); in K7g it came too late and read as a rescue |
| Tie-hedge proposal ("3 and 3, a tie still wins for you") | 5 | P7-inversion #3, S7 #2 | usually refused (Blood Price); S7 #2 a secret-ballot tie decided it |
| Fake Sage claim to point at a seat | 4 | K7 duels (Moonsage, true reads) | won once as a decoy (K7#2) |
| Motive analysis ("who wins if this seat goes?") | most Centaur wins | P7-ember #1, S7 all | the Centaurs' main second-order weapon |
| Probability argument ("a 1-in-6 coincidence") | - | G7-glamour #3, #5 | carried both frame wins |

## Prediction scorecard (honest)
- Roster level: K7 50/50 predicted, 10 C / 4 W observed; P7 C40/W30/P30 predicted, Pyre won 6 of 19 games outright or
  alongside; G7 50/50 predicted, 7 C / 4 W; X7 predicted noisy, was talk-dead but clean.
- Scenario level, 8 scenarios: winner direction right in 3 (P7-ember, S7, A3), wrong in 5 (K7-duel, G7-glamour reversed;
  K7-solo reversed; P7-inversion and A1 predicted talk-live, swept). My systematic error: I over-rated the defectors'
  credibility cards and under-rated their coordination failures and the table's backward reasoning about what each
  defector knows (hub heuristic 8, my L9 and L12).

## What is not yet known
- Natural-deal win rates: 17 natural games across 6 decks; no roster has more than 4. The class frequencies are exact
  (deal enumeration) but the per-class outcomes are mostly from 1-2 scenarios.
- Whether `packCovers` or census `allButOne` makes K7's twin duels balanced (K7 is Centaur-leaning in fixed scenarios).
- Whether the Hush helps or hurts once the harness keeps a cut player connected (G7 with the Hush is untested in fact).
- Whether a composing Lure (acting after the ember) removes P7's inversion-type sweeps, and whether allButOne tames P7's
  natural-deal scatter (only the fixed scenario was tested).
- Human tables: Opus discussions end after 80-160 s when everyone marks ready; the dynamics may differ with people.
